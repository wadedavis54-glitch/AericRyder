# Chapter 1 — Day Zero

The bus died mid-breath.

Aeric Ryder’s phone went black in his hand—*of course* it did, right when the signal bars had finally decided to exist—and then the overhead lights snapped out too. The engine cut with a sound like a throat closing.

*Okay. Power failure. Cool. Love that for us.*

For one stretched second, forty strangers sat in a rolling metal coffin that no longer believed in combustion.

Then gravity remembered them.

The bus slammed into a sedan with a hollow crunch of dead metal. Bodies whipped forward. Aeric’s forehead kissed the seatback hard enough to spark white behind his eyes. Glass spiderwebbed beside him. Something hot opened along his forearm where a shard kissed skin.

“Fuck—”

The word came out thin. The world smelled of ozone and blood and brakes that would never work again. People were already screaming. Someone yelled *bomb*. Someone else yelled *EMP*. Aeric’s brain, traitor that it was, tried to sort the options into neat little boxes while his heart tried to kick its way out of his ribs.

*Not a bomb. We’d be mist. EMP doesn’t make the air feel like…*

Then the air *filled*.

It didn’t blow. It *thickened*—grease-heavy, copper-sweet, pressing into lungs that still expected oxygen. Pressure behind the eyes. A ringing that wasn’t sound so much as the rules of the room changing without asking.

*What the hell is this? Gas? No. Gas doesn’t taste like pennies and static. Am I stroking out? Is this what a stroke feels like? Note to self: if I live, Google—*

Outside the cracked windows, the city didn’t look like a city anymore.

Streets were… stretching? Dirt where asphalt should be. Something like bone-white root clawing up through a crosswalk. A glass tower in the distance smeared sideways into mist like wet paint under a thumb.

Aeric stared until his eyes watered.

*That’s not possible. Cities don’t do that. I am definitely stroking out. Or dead. Or—*

People pounded on dead emergency strips. A man prayed loud enough to crack. A woman filmed black glass with a dead phone like ritual could restart the grid.

*Yeah, film it. That’ll help.*

His pulse was climbing, and the worst part—the part that made him want to laugh and throw up at the same time—was that under the confusion, under the *what the fuck*, something in him wasn’t only scared.

It was interested.

*Don’t be that guy. Don’t get weird about the apocalypse before you even know if it is one.*

Then the aisle unzipped.

Not metaphorically. The floor opened into cold geometry, a wound where reality had been, and every living body on the bus yanked sideways like fish on the same invisible hook. The woman across from him vanished mid-scream. The praying man dissolved into transit light.

*Oh shit oh shit oh—*

Aeric’s stomach dropped through a floor that was no longer a floor.

---

He hit nothing that should have been ground.

Dark. Thick. The grease-air worse here, packing his lungs until each breath felt stolen. He coughed, rolled onto a knee, forearm still bleeding from the crash glass, and tried to make his thoughts line up in something like order.

*Bus. Crash. Air went wrong. City went wrong. Now I’m in… space? A warehouse painted black? Hell? Be specific, brain.*

He wasn’t alone.

Something stood in front of him that was not a person. It had shape only because his mind demanded edges—cold geometry wearing the idea of a witness. Looking at it felt like looking at a spreadsheet that had learned how to hate.

When it spoke, there was no warmth. No *you’re okay*. No *remain calm*.

> **[ GUARDIAN / CONDUIT — ASSIGNMENT COMPLETE ]**
> **Status:** Permanent binding confirmed.
> **Functions:** Interface. Shop. Marketplace. Upgrade Auditor.
> **Currency:** Aether
> **Host:** Aeric Ryder | **Frame:** Mortal | **Realm:** Unforged (1st Realm)
> **Protocol:** Tutorial initialization.

“I am your Guardian,” it said, flat as instrument readout. “I will remain for the duration of your existence. I measure. I trade. I audit. I do not comfort. Planetary integration is complete. Host population is being processed. You will return to the expanded world when path selection concludes.”

Aeric blinked blood out of one eye.

*Guardian. Okay. Like an angel? Like antivirus software? Like the world’s least cuddly customer service bot?*

“I—what?” His voice came out rougher than he wanted. “Processed? Expanded *what*? Where are the other people from the bus? Where the fuck am I?”

“Individual staging,” the Guardian said. “Proximity to prior associates is not guaranteed upon ejection.”

*Individual. So everyone got yanked. Not just me. That’s… worse? Better? At least I’m not specially chosen for a cosmic prank.*

Blue overlays carved themselves into the air—numbers hanging where numbers had no right to hang.

Aeric wiped blood on his jeans and stared until the words stopped swimming.

*Name. Race. Human. Level one. Strength twelve. Dexterity eleven. Vitality fourteen. Perception thirteen. Intelligence twelve. Wisdom eleven. Like a game menu someone stapled to my soul. Except my arm is actually bleeding and that thing smells like cold metal and bad news. Is twelve good? Is ten average? Why do I have meridians? I failed high school bio and even I know that’s not how bodies work.*

“Explain,” he said. “Use small words. Pretend I’m stupid. Shouldn’t be hard.”

“Civilization substrate erased,” the Guardian said. “Planetary mass scaled. Safe havens: none. Hosts will be redistributed across the new wilderness upon ejection. Aether is universal energy—ambient fuel, Marketplace currency, and expenditure medium for cultivation and upgrades.”

*Erased. Scaled. Wilderness. Aether.*

None of it landed clean. His brain kept bouncing off the edges.

*So the city’s gone. Or bigger. Or both. And this ‘Aether’ is the thick air. And I’m supposed to… what? Level up? Pay a shop? This is insane. This is actually insane.*

And still—under the panic, under the *I don’t understand*—that wrong little spark again. Interest. Hungry. Like the part of him that had hated fluorescent mornings and polite cages had just been handed a door labeled *worse*, and some idiot piece of him wanted through it.

*Focus. Survive first. Existential horniness for the apocalypse later.*

The Guardian continued, unbothered by his pulse or his spiraling.

> **[ STABILIZATION OFFER — STANDARD TEMPLATES ]**
> **Purpose:** Handed-down scaffolds. Rapid body stabilization. Reduced mortality upon return.
> **Aether Cost:** Deferred / subsidized (scaffold lock applies).
> **Options:**
> — **Warrior** — Kinetic frame bias. Early combat reliability. Soft ceiling at mid-Realms.
> — **Weaver** — Aether channeling bias. Controlled cycling. Soft ceiling via formula lock.
> — **Warden** — Vitality bias. Defensive patterning. Soft ceiling via reactive doctrine.
> **Recommendation:** Accept a template. Most hosts will. Raw Unforged exposure without scaffold yields high fatality probability during first contact.

*Templates. Soft ceiling. Fatality probability.*

*Okay. Translation attempt: pick a starter class so you don’t die immediately, but it screws you later. Or don’t, and maybe die in the tutorial. Classic.*

He didn’t know how he knew that last part. Something behind his eyes sharpened without asking permission—patterns popping in the text like someone had circled the fine print in red.

**[Passive Trait: Analytical Eye]**

*Oh, great. I have a trait now. Cool. Love being a character sheet.*

He forced himself to slow down. Read again. Really read.

Warrior, Weaver, Warden—each one a clean little package. Easy power. Training wheels. And every description had the same quiet knife in it: *soft ceiling*. *Formula lock*. *Scaffold lock*.

*They’re selling me a cage with a warranty. Take the safe option, live longer today, stay smaller forever. Or spit on it and maybe tear myself apart.*

His hands were shaking. Adrenaline. Confusion. That dark spark again, louder.

*I’ve spent years taking the safe option. Look where that got me. A bus. A dead phone. A life so boring I was scrolling through it like a loading screen.*

“What happens if I refuse?” he asked.

“Raw exposure,” the Guardian said. “High risk. Uncapped ceiling relative to scaffolded peers. Efficient, if host survives.”

*Efficient. Not ‘brave.’ Not ‘wise.’ Efficient.*

Aeric almost laughed. Blood on his teeth.

“Fuck your templates,” he said, and heard how sure it sounded even while his stomach tried to crawl out through his shoes. “I’m not climbing into a prettier cage. Unsealed. Manual. Whatever the hard path is. If I’m doing this—and I still don’t even know what *this* is—I’m not doing it halfway.”

Silence. Not hesitation. Calculation.

> **[ PATH SELECTION ]**
> **Host rejection:** Standard templates.
> **Host demand:** Unsealed / Manual cultivation track.
> **Risk profile:** Extreme. Meridian rupture. Aether burnout. Host death on return.
> **Power ceiling:** Uncapped relative to scaffolded peers.
> **Audit verdict:** Efficient.

“Acknowledged,” the Guardian said. “High-risk selection aligns with maximum long-term output. Soft scaffolds rejected. Raw exposure commencing. Survive the Cycle, or expire before ejection.”

*Expire before ejection. What a polite way to say die in the lobby.*

“Wait—” Aeric started.

The void opened its throat.

The thick air—*Aether*, fine, whatever—hit him like a wall of molten glass.

---

Aeric folded.

Vision white. Every inch of him screamed like someone had poured battery acid into veins that had never been meant for this. Crash bruises lit up. His forearm wound went nuclear. For a stretch of time that felt like years and heartbeats at once, he was nothing but meat drowning in fuel he didn’t understand.

*Wrong choice. Wrong choice. I am an idiot. I am going to die in a black room because I wanted to be special—*

Health collapsing. Consciousness fraying.

Then something in him *clicked*—not comfort, not mercy. A stubborn, ugly refusal to shut off.

**[Innate Trait: Stubborn Will]**

The pain didn’t vanish. It *receded*—volume dialed down as the damage climbed, like his nerves had been told to file a report instead of panic.

*Okay. Okay. Still dying. Just… quieter dying. Work with that.*

The sharpened thing behind his eyes—Analytical Eye, if the floating bullshit labels could be trusted—started cutting lines through the chaos. The flood had no color, no allegiance, just pressure. Forcing it anywhere at random would shred him. He needed to *break* it down. Pull it in. Make it his instead of letting it cook him from the inside.

He didn’t have the vocabulary. He had instinct and spite.

*In. Hollow. Eat it. Don’t politely store it—devour it.*

He pressed a shaking hand to the cold pit under his navel—empty, aware, hungry in a way that should have terrified him—and pulled.

**[Manual Technique: Null Cycling — Lvl 1]**

It scraped.

Swallowing broken glass through veins that had forgotten they were pipes. He hauled the void’s grease-thick flood into himself and *broke* it—stripped heat, stripped foreign signatures, stripped everything until what remained was Null, neutral, mean. Something tore open inside him—three paths of fire from wrist to ribs to that hollow under his gut. The rest of him stayed shut, locked, useless.

*Meridians. That’s what the screen meant. One hundred eight. I opened three. Holy shit. Holy—*

“Shit—” The word ripped out of him as another channel tried and failed, searing him hollow. Stubborn Will climbed with the damage. He tracked the compression like a man counting cards in a burning casino: too fast and it vents; too slow and it cooks him. Whatever Vitality 14 meant, it bought seconds. He spent them.

*This is the worst thing I’ve ever felt. This is also the first thing that’s felt real in years. What is wrong with me?*

His crash cut hissed where friction burned the edges. The hollow under his gut flickered. A thin gaseous film. Unstable. Pathetic.

Numbers updated in the air like the universe had a clipboard.

1.2%.

*A start. A stupid, tiny, agonizing start.*

The Guardian watched without blinking, if blinking was even a thing it did.

“I still don’t know what any of this—”

The void dropped him.

---

Aeric hit dirt that had never been a street.

The sky was too wide. The trees looked like spears. Mist thick enough to taste sat on his tongue—the same grease-copper wrongness, Aether, and knowing the word didn’t make it less insane. He scrambled up, spinning, looking for the bus, the road, a single fucking streetlight.

Nothing.

Distant screams. Human. Close enough to hear. Too far to reach.

*Scattered. It said scattered. Everyone got dumped alone into… this. Wherever this is.*

Across a shallow ravine, a man in a bloodied office shirt stood frozen, some kind of light flickering uselessly around his fists—Warrior, maybe, if Aeric was guessing right—and a stone-hide beast closed the distance while the man did nothing but stare.

*Move. MOVE—*

The monster took his leg at the hip. Bone cracked wet and loud. Arterial blood sprayed the roots. The man finally screamed when the second bite opened his belly and steamed ropes of gut hit the dirt.

Aeric’s stomach clenched.

His pulse slammed.

Fear was there—of course it was, he wasn’t made of concrete—but it didn’t get the steering wheel. What got the steering wheel was adrenaline, sharp and clean, and under it that dark excitement he still didn’t have a healthy name for. Soft people freezing. Paying for a world that had taught them waiting would save them.

*Jesus. I’m watching a man get turned into hamburger and part of me is—what? Impressed? Awake? What the fuck is wrong with me?*

Farther upslope, someone fought well—a woman forcing shimmering spears of that thick air into crude points, keeping two lesser shapes off a kid who actually seemed to know how to brace. Competent. Alive. For now.

*So templates work. Until they don’t. Good data. Horrible TED talk.*

His hands felt empty. Stupidly empty. The ravine floor was littered with broken branches from whatever the expansion had done to the trees—bone-pale, snapped clean. Aeric grabbed the longest shard within reach: a snapped spear of deadwood, rough enough to tear his palm, pointed enough to matter.

*Not a sword. Not a plan. Better than fingernails.*

Something low and wrong burst from the brush toward *him*—too many joints, jaws hinged for tearing.

A label burned into his vision without asking.

**[Scan]**
> **Entity:** Aether-Warped Cur
> **Level:** 2
> **Threat:** Low-tier field spawn

*Level two. I’m level one. Math says I die. Math can get in line.*

He didn’t freeze.

He moved—half instinct, half the Eye carving ugly little advantages out of panic, the deadwood spear already in his fist because he’d been smart enough to pick it up while other people were still deciding whether to scream. The half-step turned a throat-bite into a forearm rake. Claws opened meat to bone. Pain tried to own him; Stubborn Will turned it into information. He slammed the Cur into a root hard enough to feel something give, jammed his ruined arm into its teeth—*stupid, necessary, fuck*—and drove the deadwood up through the soft place under its jaw.

Wet crunch. Arterial spray across his mouth. The Cur thrashed, claws scoring his ribs and thigh in dying spasms—then went still.

Aeric knelt in the blood-mud, shaking so hard his teeth clicked.

*I killed it. I killed a—whatever that was. I’m bleeding. I’m laughing. Stop laughing. No, keep laughing. I don’t know. I don’t know anything.*

Somewhere else a scream cut short. His wounds were very real. The hollow under his gut flickered, and Null Cycling tugged on instinct—pulling a trickle of the Cur’s dying whatever-it-was into himself, burning the edges of torn meat as fuel.

Thriving wasn’t the word.

Not safe. Not clean. Not enlightened.

Just alive in a way that made the bus, the phone, the soft gray life before this feel like a waiting room he’d finally left—without knowing what building he’d walked into.
