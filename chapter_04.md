# Chapter 4 — Bad Water

Thirst arrived before hunger.

Not polite thirst—the kind that sat behind his teeth and made every swallow he *didn't* take feel like a mistake. The wash from the hound fight had been cold and iron-sick and full of blood. He'd crouched in it. He hadn't drunk.

*Siphon isn't water. Mariusz said that without saying it. The rush feels like yes and my mouth still tastes like copper rot.*

The forest kept getting wrong.

Trees grew through metal the way roots grew through bone—slow violence frozen mid-chew. A car door jutted from a trunk at shoulder height, paint peeled to gray primer, handle still attached like a joke. Farther on, a bent signpost buried to the waist, letters shredded but the ghost of white rectangle still there. Commute scenery. Office hours. None of it where it belonged.

*The bus was east. Or north. Or the map in my head died with the phone.*

Analytical Eye tried to measure distance and got nonsense back—horizon too wide, sound carrying too clean, gaps between trunks wide enough for things he didn't have names for yet. Primeval density, the Interface had called it. Resolving. Like the world was still deciding what to call the knife it had turned itself into.

His tongue stuck to the roof of his mouth.

*Water first. Then whatever wants to eat me second.*

---

The seep showed itself as smell before sight—wet stone, rot, something sweet underneath that didn't belong in clean water.

Aeric knelt where two root-walls pinched a slice of shade. Thin film of liquid threaded down moss into a cup of hollowed stone no bigger than his two hands. Clear enough to lie. When he leaned close, the copper Aether-taste in the air doubled, and black flecks swam in the meniscus like ash that couldn't decide to sink.

"Don't," Mariusz said.

"I'm not an idiot."

"You are thirsty, injured, and about to be an idiot. Those are different failure modes. Listen anyway."

Aeric's jaw worked. He didn't drink.

"This is not your city's tap. Warped runoff, beast piss upstream, dissolved Aether sediment from things that died in the loam—maybe human, maybe not. Unforged meat drinks that and invites parasites, gut rot, fever. Your Vitality buys slack, not immunity."

*Great. Apocalypse hydration tier list.*

"If you had fire and time—boil it. Rolling boil drives off most surface biologics. It does **not** purify Aether-bound taint. Nothing in a nursery forest does, short of Copper Core stability or a filter you haven't built. Boiling is still better than raw. You have neither fire nor patience."

"I have thirst."

"Then you choose: dehydration collapse in six to ten hours at your current bleed rate, or controlled risk now. Sip. Small. Let your stomach vote. If it revolts, you will know before you drown in it."

Aeric stared at the stone cup.

*Wade never let me quit on the mat because I was tired. He'd say drink between rounds or don't complain when your head clears wrong.*

He cupped both hands under the seep and caught what he could. Warm-ish. Metallic. Smelled like a hospital corridor that had been left to mold—antiseptic ghost under the rot.

*Maria hated hospitals.*

One line. Gone.

He sipped.

It tasted like pennies and bad decisions. His stomach clenched, considered rebellion, and—after a brutal minute—didn't. The sip became two. Three. Not enough. Enough to keep walking.

"Record that," Mariusz said. "Bad water kept you alive. Good water is a project. Boil when you can. Until then, every source is negotiation."

*Negotiation. Sure. I'll shake hands with the piss upstream later.*

---

The shelter found him, not the other way around.

A bus carcass lay on its side in a trough of torn earth—yellow paint scabbed off, windows blown to glitter in the loam. One ripped panel made a lean-to against root-mat; inside, dim green light and air that didn't carry his blood scent to every nose in the county.

*Commute. Forty strangers. Phone dead mid-scream.*

He didn't let the memory finish. Memory didn't stop the sediment grinding in his hollow.

Four open channels and the grit was still there—less than after the partial burn in the wash, but present. Trace load. Mariusz had said a full flush needed an hour he hadn't had with a hound sniffing downslope.

He had shade now. And time that might be a lie.

"Full burn," Mariusz said. "You don't know this yet—"

"I know the steps."

"You know the **partial** steps. Listen anyway. Sediment flush is not one push. It is cycling mud through every open meridian until the hollow stops feeling like sand in a gearbox. Partial kept you moving. Full keeps you from rupturing when you leech again."

Aeric sat with his back to cold metal that might have been a seat frame once. Spear across his thighs. Hands on his stomach.

"Step one. Find the hollow. You live there now. Good."

*Yeah.*

"Step two. Ambient draw—thin breath. Grease-thick air through all **four** open paths. The draw does not hurt. If you gulp, you pack new sludge atop old. Thin."

He pulled.

Cool neutral ease slid into channels still scraped raw from Channel Four's birth. No corpse under his palm. No reward-rush. Just air, and the grit already inside scraping back.

"Step three. Stop drawing. The poison is yours to evict, not the world's to replace."

He cut the pull.

"Step four. Spin outward. Push the Cycle through each open meridian in sequence—one, two, three, four—then vent. Friction burn. Molten-wire drag. That is sediment, not Opening, not leech-pleasure. Again. Until the hollow stops crunching."

He pushed.

Heat. Agony with no mind to hate—dead weight fighting eviction. Stubborn Will climbed the load and kept him counting. First meridian: burn, vent, spit of something wrong leaving his breath. Second: worse, then better. Third: his forearm cuts wept fresh as friction pulled blood to the surface. Fourth: the channel that had screamed open yesterday held under pressure.

Again.

The fourth pass broke something loose—not a new path, a **last** clot in the stack. Aeric bent forward and gagged air that tasted like hot pennies and old oil. The stone cup outside the lean-to rippled though nothing had touched it.

Again—lighter.

Again—almost clean.

Aeric sat in the dim until his hands stopped shaking.

"An hour," he breathed. "You weren't kidding."

"I am rarely kidding about mud in your meridians." Mariusz paused. "You are clean enough to leech without immediate rupture risk. That is not permission to be stupid about cores."

*Residue first. Got it. Forever, apparently.*

The forest answered with silence that lasted almost long enough to believe.

Then—twig snap. Soft foot. Wrong weight distribution.

Not beetle click. Not hound snuffle.

**[Scan]**

<blockquote class="status-block">

<p><strong>Level:</strong> 2</p>

<p><strong>Threat:</strong> Solo ambusher — soft throat seam; thorn-armored shoulders; pounce / pin predator</p>

</blockquote>

*Level two. Solo. Soft throat. Same homework, different shape.*

It came over the bus roof without sound—briar-matted shoulders, face too flat, eyes like wet glass. Pounce math: Aeric still on his ass, spear length wrong, exit blocked by root-wall.

Impact.

Eighty kilos of wrong cat drove him into metal. Jaws snapped where his face had been a heartbeat ago. Claws raked his outer arm—new hot lines through shirt and skin—and something thorny caught his thigh **on the old wound**, reopening what the hound had started.

*Fuck—*

His back hit earth. Weight on his chest. Stink of sap and blood.

*Wade's voice, ten years old and mean as love:* **Don't give your back.**

He hadn't. Shoulders flat, hips angled—not perfect, rusted, but the frame was there. The Bramble-Cat pressed for the throat and Aeric **framed** on its jaw with his forearm, elbow tight to ribs, making space the way a kid on a mat made space when someone heavier thought gravity was an argument.

*Hip escape. Now. Don't bridge stupid.*

He shrimped—awkward, bleeding, metal biting his scalp—and slid his hips out from under the pin an inch. Then two. The cat adjusted, claws digging for purchase on his belly, and Aeric turned into it, got a knee between them, **framed** again.

The spear had skittered arm's reach away.

*Weapon discipline. Black belt bullshit. Move.*

He grabbed the haft, pulled it in, and used the butt to jab thorn-shoulder where armor lived. The cat hissed. Aeric rode the recoil up to half-mount—not clean, not pretty, weight on its upper chest, forearm seeking the soft seam under the jaw where Scan said the throat was soft.

The Bramble-Cat bucked. Claws found his ribs. Stubborn Will turned it into countdown numbers.

*Three seconds. Two.*

He drove deadwood down into the throat seam.

Wet give. Not deep enough—armor ridge caught the slide. Aeric adjusted, **re-gripped** like a collar choke setup without the choke, and punched the point through on the second angle.

The cat shuddered. Blood hot on his knuckles. Dying. Not dead.

Separate beat.

Palm off the wood. Flat against the soft seam at the side of the neck. Bare skin. Direct contact. Deliberate.

Null Cycling woke through the touch.

The rush hit—dark, warm, **good**, a bloom of yes behind his sternum that made his breath catch like after a win he didn't deserve. Life-Aether fled the cat's throat and poured up his arm before it could scatter into the bus metal. Three heartbeats of chase. Three heartbeats of *more* whispering under the good.

Something denser pulsed beneath his palm—the core-knot, the seed.

Aeric **ripped back**.

Withdrawal snapped cold through the heat—wrong, empty, almost grief—and the cat went still.

He sat astride a cooling corpse breathing hard, palm twitching with want.

*I stopped. Again. That should feel like winning. Why does stopping feel like loss?*

"Control," Mariusz said flatly. "Not virtue. Residue bought fuel without another sediment flood. You have room for one reconstruction if you spend it now—thigh's reopening will slow you by nightfall otherwise."

*Spend it. Painful Reconstruction. The part that's supposed to hurt.*

"Do it," Aeric said.

"Hand on the wound. Pull the stolen vitality **out** of the hollow—not leech-in, spend-out. Molten wire through torn meat. You will hate me. Execute."

He pressed his palm to the raked thigh and **pushed** bound Null the other direction.

Agony.

Not the leech-high. Not sediment burn. **Reconstruction**— stolen life forced to re-stitch what claws had opened, wire-hot, every fiber screaming as it knit wrong-fast. Aeric locked his teeth and rode it until the worst of the bleed stopped and the pain peaked and dropped like a stage curtain.

*The theft felt good. The healing feels like punishment. Of course it does.*

"Would you rather limp into night with an open thigh and no fuel?"

*Fair. Still hate it.*

"Channel five," Mariusz said. "While the Cycle is warm and the mud is only trace. I will count. You will not show off."

---

He dragged himself back under the lean-to, out of the blood scent cone, spine to metal.

"Ambient draw. Thin."

Easy pull. Neutral air.

"Find the fifth seam. Deeper clog—older lock. Your body has ninety-nine more excuses after this one."

The Eye found it—tight band of scar-tissue wrapped around something that felt like a pebble in a hose.

"Push. Meridian Opening is friction pain. Separate from leech-pleasure. Separate from sediment. Separate from reconstruction. Too fast ruptures. Too slow resets. Hold—now—"

Pain arrived with opinions.

Mechanical. Birth-wrong. Like forcing a route through meat that had never meant to carry this much load.

Stubborn Will climbed. Mariusz counted. Aeric pushed on three and four and **held** on five when every instinct said sprint.

The clog **cracked**.

Fire ran from hollow to chest—not pleasure, not mud, **passage**—until a fifth channel tore open one brutal inch at a time and the Cycle poured through without backing up behind four overloaded wires.

He screamed into his closed teeth until air mattered more.

The channel **held**.

"Five," Aeric panted. "In one day. Say something that isn't Keller."

Mariusz was quiet a beat. "Keller would still be on three channels, paying scaffold interest, praying his Weaver cords don't snap his wrists. You paid in bad water, full burn, ground game, and a cat that almost had your throat. Do not waste the math by dying of pride."

*There it is. Name like a thumb on a bruise.*

---

He left the bus grave when the light through the torn panel shifted wrong—too long, too green, no sun he could trust.

The forest thinned into a slope of ferns and half-buried railing that might have been a motorway once. Voices didn't travel here. Screams did.

One tore through the trees ahead—human, female, cut off mid syllable like scissors through a throat.

Aeric stopped. Spear up. Analytical Eye hunting source.

Silence.

Then wet tearing. Something feeding.

He moved toward the sound because stupidity and curiosity shared a bed, and stopped on a ridge overlooking a shallow bowl of crushed ferns.

Not Jonah.

A woman in office flats and a torn blazer, spread wrong in the loam. Belly opened. A pair of low jointed things—beetle-lings, carapace still soft, not full Carrion size—worked the cavity with mandibles while something else's ichor dried black on her collar.

Still twitching when he watched.

Not long for anything but food.

*Help—*

The word tried to attach to Jonah's face. Keller's luminous cords popping like wet string. Aeric's feet choosing the downslope path that **didn't** lead to her.

*I have a spear and five channels and bad water in my stomach. Those things aren't level three. They're not my math. Neither was Jonah.*

The smaller scavengers hadn't Scanned him yet. If he went in, noise would travel. More would come. His thigh would reopen. Sediment would pack.

*Could've been anyone. Wasn't. Isn't.*

The woman's hand twitched once. No weave. No template glow. Just fingers clawing fern-mat for a grip that wasn't there.

Aeric's jaw locked.

*Cohen would call me a coward. Wade would ask if I had odds. I don't.*

He backed into the ferns. One step. Two. Silent as a man who had learned blood scent was a dinner bell.

The scream didn't resume. The wet tearing continued.

Mariusz said nothing for thirty paces. When he spoke, it was barely sound. "You will carry that. Good. Carry it where it makes you faster, not where it makes you dead."

*Jonah's still out there. Or isn't. This one's already inventory.*

He didn't answer. The forest swallowed the bowl behind him—primeval density closing, metal ghosts underfoot, thirst muted but not gone.

Five open channels. Trace sediment. Seventeen currency units he couldn't spend in a shop that didn't exist.

*Residue first. Boil when you can. Don't die for strangers the math can't save.*

He repeated it like a prayer he was learning to mean and walked toward whatever came next.
