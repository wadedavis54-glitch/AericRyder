# Chapter 5 — First Fire

Night didn't arrive like night.

The light thinned wrong—green bleed through the bus panel, no stars, no moon he trusted, just a dimmer version of the same wrong afternoon that had been pretending to be a day. Aeric sat in the lean-to with the spear across his knees and listened to his body file complaints in order.

Thirst first. Not collapse-thirst. The bad seep had bought hours. His tongue still felt like cardboard.

Hunger second. A cramp under his ribs that had nothing to do with the hollow and everything to do with **meat he hadn't eaten in a day he couldn't count**.

*Siphon isn't food. Mariusz said that. The rush lied anyway—felt fed, still empty.*

Third: the arm the Bramble-Cat had opened. Clotting, not closed. Fourth: the old Cur cuts. Fifth: exhaustion stacked like bad debt.

Five open channels hummed faintly when he breathed too deep—new passage still angry at being used. Trace sediment ground in the hollow. Not crisis. Not clean.

*Boil when you can. You can't. Not yet.*

He didn't sleep. He **waited**, which was worse.

"What was that?" Aeric muttered.

"Your body admitting five channels carry more than four," Mariusz said. "The Interface calls it Perception. I call it *you might notice the thing that kills you half a second sooner*. Don't get romantic about it."

*Half a second. Sure. I'll take it.*

The forest breathed. Wet. Close. Metal ghost under the root-mat where the bus had died.

*Commute. Forty strangers. Phone dead.*

He stood. Joints protested. The thigh held—reconstruction money well spent, even if paying it had felt like punishment for the theft that felt good.

"Project one," Mariusz said. "Fire."

"I don't have matches."

"You have wreckage, desperation, and me. Matches would make you stupid faster."

*Fair.*

---

"You don't know this yet. Listen."

Aeric stopped rummaging through the bus gut and listened.

"Fire is a ladder. Tinder, then kindling, then fuel. Skip a rung and you fall. Wind kills small flame—build where the panel blocks draft, not where blood scent broadcasts dinner."

The lean-to was wrong for wind and right for shame. He worked inside the torn cavity.

"Step one. **Dry tinder.** Not green. Not damp loam. Yellow seat foam from the bench frame—tear it fine. Shredded paper if you find it. Lint from clothing. You want a bird's nest, not a brick."

He ripped foam until his fingers ached. Found a water-swollen commuter mag—pages peeled in strips. A dead phone in the aisle, useless. No miracle lighter in a corpse's pocket. He checked anyway. Nothing but keys and a wallet full of cards that meant less than the foam.

"Step two. **Kindling.** Pencil-thin. Split deadwood from the spear's shadow—don't wreck your weapon yet. Splinters. Dry bark if the root-mat offers it."

He broke kindling with the spear butt until his palms wept old cuts.

"Step three. **Spark source.** Chrome door handle against quartz aggregate from the shattered window line. Hard strike, glancing angle—steel bites stone, not smashes it. You will fail first. Failure is data."

*Wade would say drill it until it's boring.*

He failed six times. Sparks died in damp thumbs. On the seventh, a orange fleck caught in the foam nest and **died**.

"Breath control. Not a hurricane. Cup it. Feed kindling when it whispers yes."

Eighth strike. The nest **smoked**.

Aeric breathed like he was back on a mat between rounds—slow, stupid, careful—and fed a splinter. Flame.

Small. Ugly. **Real.**

"Hold it," Mariusz said. "Build the ladder before you celebrate."

He did. Kindling caught. Then a seat frame shard. Then thicker root-mat deadfall dragged into the cavity mouth where smoke could vent without putting out the base.

*Fire. Actual fire. I could boil water. I could not freeze.*

The triumph lasted three seconds before hunger reminded him fire also meant **cook**, and cook meant **meat**, and meat meant something else's dying math.

He sat a while without moving.

Not rest. **Inventory.** Flame in the cavity mouth. Smoke threading up through torn metal. His hands still shook from the eighth strike—not fear, fine motor backlash, the body admitting it had been hours since sleep and longer since food. The forest pressed close but didn't rush him. Nothing crunched in the fern line. No mandibles. No cat weight on a bus roof.

For one wrong minute the apocalypse was only **warmth** and the smell of burning foam that had once been someone's commute seat.

*Don't get used to it. Warmth is fuel leaving. Fuel you don't have much of.*

He fed the flame one splinter anyway and watched it catch, because stupid hope was still a resource.

---

"Project two," Mariusz said. "Water—again. With heat this time."

Aeric carried the stone cup from yesterday's seep—same moss-thread, same ash flecks in the meniscus when he refilled it. The flame under the cup wasn't a kitchen. It was survival cosplay.

"Rolling boil. Bubbles that don't lie. One minute after full boil if you can afford fuel. You cannot afford much—make it count."

The water roiled. Pennies-and-rot smell turned to steam that hit his face like a slap he welcomed.

*Maria hated hospitals. Steam still smells like clean if you don't think about it.*

He let it boil. Counted sixty. Let it cool enough not to peel his throat.

He drank.

Better. Not good. Not pure. Mariusz had been clear—**boil drives surface biologics, not Aether-bound taint**. Nothing in this forest was clean until a stable Core or a filter he didn't have.

"Record it," Mariusz said. "Boiled bad water beats raw bad water. Thirst buys another day. **Not** immunity. **Not** permission to drink every puddle you meet."

*Negotiation upgraded. Fine.*

He filled the cup again and set it aside—reserve. The fire ate kindling faster than he liked.

*Food. Now.*

---

The berries looked edible the way lies looked sincere.

Fat purple clusters on a low bush near the bus trough—sun-side, plump, the kind of thing a office lunch break fantasy put in a plastic clamshell.

Aeric's hand stopped an inch away.

"Don't," Mariusz said.

"I'm hungry, not dead."

"Those are **close cousins of dead.** Warped fruit concentrates Aether runoff—beast piss upstream, rot in the root web, sediment in the loam. Unforged gut eats that and you get fever, gut twist, parasite bloom. Your Vitality buys **slack**, not immunity. I said that about water. I'll say it until you listen."

*Listen.*

He pulled his hand back. Stomach cramped harder, like the body knew he'd almost betrayed it.

"Forage fail is data," Mariusz went on, voice dry as the foam that had caught flame. "When you kill something **you choose to eat**, you avoid glands and ichor sacks, you **burn** surface warp-taint, you cook until the center isn't pink slime. Raw beast on this world is a lottery where every ticket loses. **Cores are not food.** Cores are knots for later—fuel, craft, breakthrough pressure—not breakfast."

*Foundation Siphon. Cores. Later.*

"Can I eat anything in this fucking forest?"

"Eventually. Today you earn **meat** the way you earn channels—blood first, theory second. Fire third. You're two for three."

*Cohen would laugh at that. If he's alive.*

Aeric turned from the berries and went downslope toward wetter ground where the seep fed a darker pool.

Something moved in the mud.

It didn't announce. Mud erupted. The eel was longer than his arm—segmented, oil-slick, mouth a puckered ring of needle teeth. It hit his calf like a wet belt sander and **coiled** upward, dorsal ridge grinding cloth into skin.

Aeric stabbed down. Spear point skidded on armor.

*Not the back. Belly.*

He dropped weight, pinned the coil with his knee the way he'd pinned a training partner's hip—not pretty, **rusty**, but the angle opened.

*Finish on the mat. Don't rush when it's still moving.*

Wade's voice, old and mean as love. Aeric exhaled once through the pressure and shoved the spear under the segment lift where Scan said ventral was soft and **drove**.

Give. Ichor, not blood—black-green and hot.

The coil loosened. The eel thrashed. Dying. Not dead.

Separate beat.

Palm off the wood. Flat against the soft ventral seam where the segment parted wrong. Bare skin. Direct contact. Deliberate.

The rush hit—dark, warm, **good**, a pulse of yes that made his knees weak for half a heartbeat. Life-Aether fled the eel's meat before it could bleed into the pool. He chased three beats. Stopped on four when something **dense** pulsed under the skin—the core-knot.

He **ripped back**.

Cold where heat had been. Wrong. Empty.

The eel went still in the mud.

*Residue. Not the seed. Why does stopping always feel like loss?*

"Control," Mariusz said. "Trace sediment. Cook first—the trace is light. **Flush before the next leech**, not before calories. And **do not** drink the pool."

"I wasn't going to drink the pool."

"You were thirsty enough to consider worse."

*Fair.*

He banked the coals higher and found the hollow under his gut before his hands went back to the corpse.

"Thin draw. Outward spin. One path at a time—all **five**. Vent. That is sediment, not Opening. Quick. Ugly. Enough."

Friction burn licked through the new channel—molten-wire drag, dead weight evicted in three small vents that tasted wrong on his tongue. The hollow stopped **crunching**. Not clean. Clean enough to eat without packing mud into his gut on top of eel.

*Cook. Then survive the next mistake.*

---

Butchery without a skill was ugly work.

Mariusz talked him through it like a lab dissection—**no glands near the head junction**, strip the ventral strip where muscle was thickest, **discard** the ichor line, wash in boiled water not raw pool. Aeric's hands shook. The fire had burned low; he fed it until the flame steadied.

"Skewer. Char the surface. Center still gross is **expected**. Unforged you need calories, not cuisine. Chew slow. If your gut revolts in twenty minutes, you will know."

The meat smelled like mud and copper and something sweet that shouldn't exist. He ate half standing over his own fire like an animal who'd learned to use tools.

It tasted **awful**.

It tasted like **staying alive**.

His stomach unclenched—not full, not satisfied, but **fed** in the way that mattered. The leech-rush had lied before. This was honest ugly.

*First cooked beast. Gross. Necessary. File that.*

"Half for later," Mariusz said. "Smoke if you can. You won't master smoke today. Wrap in clean-ish leaf, eat before the next night if nothing eats you first."

*Optimist.*

---

"Channel six," Mariusz said when the fire was banked and the sediment itch was a whisper, not a scream. "While the Cycle is warm. You don't know the sixth seam yet—"

"I know the steps."

"You know **five** of them. Listen for the sixth anyway."

Aeric sat spine to bus metal. Spear across thighs.

"Ambient draw. Thin. Five paths, not a gulp."

Easy air. Neutral. No corpse. No reward.

"Find the sixth clog. Deeper than five—body's running out of easy excuses."

The Eye found a knot behind his solar plexus—tight, old, **mean**.

"Push. Meridian Opening is friction pain. Not leech-pleasure. Not sediment. Not reconstruction. Too fast ruptures. Too slow resets. Hold—now—"

Pain.

Mechanical. Birth-wrong. Wire through meat that had never meant to carry load.

Stubborn Will climbed. Mariusz counted. Aeric pushed on three and **held** on four when every instinct said sprint.

The knot **cracked**.

Passage tore open inch by inch. Fire ran hollow to ribs—not pleasure, not mud, **capacity**—until a sixth channel held and the Cycle poured through without backing up behind five overloaded wires.

He breathed through his teeth until air mattered more.

"In two days that **are** a week by your body's ledger. Do not compare your channel count to scaffold children who buy width with ceiling."

*Keller. Of course.*

---

He left the bus grave when the boiled reserve was packed in a hollowed leaf wrap and the fire was dead ash he could hide.

The slope thinned. Ferns. Railing ghost under loam. Sound carried wrong—too far, too clean.

A voice ahead. Male. Raw.

"Hello? **Hello?**"

Human.

Aeric stopped. Spear up. Perception—new half-second or not—**listened** for the shape behind the word.

"Anyone—please—"

Crunch. Wet. Not a reply.

Silence.

*Not Jonah. Wrong timber. Wrong direction.*

Mariusz said nothing.

Aeric didn't move toward the sound. His feet knew the math before his pride finished arguing—six channels, trace sediment, half a meal in leaf wrap, no template glow to trust, no odds that improved because someone said hello.

His weight shifted toward the voice anyway. Ball of foot. Knee bending. One step that would become two—

He stopped.

The half-step cost more than backing away would. His knuckles went white on the spear haft before he made himself loose them.

*Could've been anyone. Wasn't. Isn't.*

He backed into the ferns. Silent. Blood scent on his cuffs from the eel. Fire ash on his knuckles.

The forest didn't answer the hello again.
