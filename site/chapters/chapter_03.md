# Chapter 3 — Sediment

The forest didn’t get quieter the farther he went.

It got *organized*.

Sound came in layers—drip, click, something heavy moving through understory that wasn’t him. Aeric kept the deadwood spear low and his bleeding quiet, which was a joke his body didn’t find funny. Every step reopened the half-seals on his forearm and ribs. The shin rake from the beetle fight had stiffened into a hot line that pulled wrong when he bent his knee.

*Walk. Don’t limp louder than the things hunting. Great plan. Love the biome.*

The hollow under his gut—the Dantian, if he was going to use Mariusz’s vocabulary like it belonged to him—had stopped feeling empty.

It felt **gritty**.

Not pain. Not the good dark rush from touching dying meat. Something worse: the sensation of sand dragged through a wound that was supposed to hold fuel, clogging the three narrow meridian paths he’d torn open in the void like hair in a drain.

*What the fuck is that.*

“Impure Aether Sediment,” Mariusz said. His voice stayed behind Aeric’s eyes, dry as chalk dust. “You gorged on a dying beetle like a drowning man. You almost ate a core whole. Low-tier kills deposit sludge when you drink past residue. It clogs what little you’ve opened. Keep siphoning like that and your meridians will rupture before your ambition does.”

Aeric’s jaw tightened.

*Sediment. Of course there’s sediment. Why wouldn’t the apocalypse have sand in the fuel tank.*

“Can I—”

“Burn it off. Manual cycling. Agony without the high. No shortcut. No shop. You want the devouring path, you learn to digest what you steal—or choke on it.”

Analytical Eye tried to map it: Cur trickle, beetle flood, overdraw on the knot he’d almost taken. Two kills. Two rushes that felt too good. One price he hadn’t seen on the menu.

*Residue first. He said that after. Thanks for the post-game tutorial.*

“How long do I have before I’m a clogged pipe?” Aeric muttered.

“Long enough to make one more mistake if you’re stupid. Short enough that I would not plan a nap.”

*Cool. Cool cool cool.*

---

He found a fold in the terrain—a shallow wash where runoff had cut between two root-walls, knee-deep with cold water that smelled of iron and rotted leaf. Not clean. Better than standing in his own blood scent on open ground.

Aeric crouched behind the upstream root and looked at his ruined palm.

The splinters were still there, black at the edges. Infection waiting for permission. He didn’t have bandages. He didn’t have soap. He had copper-thick air and a demon scholar who thought pain was pedagogy.

*Later.*

He reached for the hollow under his gut and pulled.

Null Cycling. Ambient Aether this time—no corpse under his hand, no dying warmth to make the draw feel like a reward. Just the grease-thick air scraped down his arm through channels that were already overloaded and now carried **grit**.

It felt like swallowing broken glass with sand in it.

Stubborn Will climbed the damage load and kept him conscious while the sediment resisted. Not a living thing. Worse—a sludge with no mind, no fleeing life to catch, just dead weight that wanted to stay where it was choking him.

*Burn. Mariusz said burn.*

He forced the Cycle faster—not pulling in more, *pushing* what was already inside through the three open meridians like trying to flush a clogged pipe with molten wire.

Heat. Friction. The good rush never came.

Somewhere in the grind, one grainy blockage **broke**.

Not a meridian opening—a clog giving way inside a path he’d already paid for. The release was small and ugly, like spitting something that had no right to be in his mouth. The water in the wash rippled though nothing had touched it.

> **[ CYCLE AUDIT ]**
> **Process:** Sediment Burn — Manual Flush
> **Result:** Partial clearance — trace Impure Aether Sediment remains
> **Note:** Complete burn before next kill-contact siphon. Overdraw on core-bearing targets will compound load.

“Partial,” Aeric breathed. “Love partial. Partial is my favorite word today.”

Mariusz’s exhale was almost amusement. Almost. “Partial keeps you alive. Complete would have taken an hour you do not have with that scent trail behind you. You flushed enough to move. Do not confuse survival with cleanliness.”

*Scent trail. Right. I’m a walking lunch menu.*

A sound answered from downslope—wet snuffle, claws on stone, something big enough to make the root-wall vibrate under Aeric’s palm.

Not clicking beetles.

One set of legs.

---

**[Scan]**
> **Entity:** Aether-Warped Gnarl-Hound
> **Level:** 2
> **Threat:** Solo scavenger — soft gut seam; armored spine and skull

*Level two. Solo. Soft gut. Same homework as everything else wants to kill me.*

The hound came around the root-bend like it owned the wash—shoulders corded wrong, spine plated with bark-colored bone, muzzle wet with old blood that wasn’t Aeric’s. It smelled him and went still.

For one heartbeat they measured each other.

Aeric didn’t have pack math this time. No Jonah Keller bleeding luminous cords against a trunk. No second monster to choose the easier meal.

Just him, the spear, and a body already half-used.

*Residue first. Leave the core. Don’t drink the mud.*

The hound lunged.

He didn’t meet it square—meeting it square was how office-shirt man lost his leg across the ravine. Aeric slid downslope into the cold wash, water exploding around his knees, and let the hound’s mass overshoot on the slick stone. Stubborn Will turned the forearm tear into data. The Eye found the seam: belly, soft, where the armored spine didn’t wrap.

He drove deadwood up under the ribs.

Not deep enough first pass—the hound twisted, jaws snapping air where his face had been, claw raking his outer thigh and opening a fresh hot line through jeans and skin. Aeric snarled, planted a boot against stone, and **shoved**.

The spear punched through.

Wet give. A stink like opened gut and copper. The hound bucked, trying to bite the wood still inside it, and Aeric rode the motion down into the wash until the beast’s hindquarters hit the bank and stopped fighting gravity.

Dying. Not dead yet.

The gap opened—the same stupid instinct his hand had learned on the Cur without permission.

This time he **chose** it.

Palm flat against the soft seam around the spear, fingers sinking into hot wet fur and ruined meat. Bare skin. Direct contact. Separate from the killing blow.

Null Cycling woke through the touch.

The rush hit—dark, warm, *good*, a bloom of yes behind his sternum that made his teeth want to grin at the wrong moment. Life-Aether fled the hound’s gut and poured up his arm before it could scatter into the wash, and for three heartbeats it felt like the beetle again, like the first hit of something he already knew he’d chase.

*Stop. Residue. Not the knot. Not the—*

Something denser pulsed under his palm—a seed, a core, the hard little knot Mariusz had warned him about.

Aeric **ripped his hand back** before his Dantian could lunge.

The pull cut off mid-rush. Withdrawal snapped through him like cold after heat—wrong, empty, almost grief—and the hound shuddered once and went still.

He sat in the wash breathing hard, water pink around his knees, palm shaking with want.

*I stopped. I actually stopped.*

“Good,” Mariusz said, and the word was tight with something that might have been relief if he were the type. “That is what control looks like—not virtue. You left the core because I told you to, and because you still want meridians tomorrow. The residue alone bought you fuel without another sediment flood. Remember the difference. Residue is spill. The core is foundation. Foundation Siphon is when you learn to take the seed without drinking the mud. You are not there yet.”

*Foundation Siphon. File that. Don’t touch the knot. Got it.*

The hound’s body cooled in the wash. Aeric didn’t look at its eyes.

He looked at his Interface instead, because numbers were easier than what his hand still wanted.

*Channel four. The one that told me no.*

He dragged himself onto the bank, out of the pink water, back against the root-wall where the hound couldn’t reach him from the grave. Spear across his thighs. Palm still twitching with the ghost of the rush.

Null Cycling. Pull ambient Aether—careful, slower, the sediment grit still scraping the insides of the three open paths like sand on glass.

He found the seam Mariusz had pointed at before: scar tissue wrapped around clog, the fourth channel refusing to exist because his body had never believed it needed to.

*Push.*

Pain arrived with opinions.

Not the leech-high. Not the molten-wire reconstruction burn. Meridian friction—pure, mechanical, like forcing a route through meat that had been locked since birth.

Stubborn Will climbed. The Eye tracked pressure: too fast and something ruptures; too slow and the sediment resettles.

The clog **cracked**.

Fire ran from the hollow under his gut up through his chest—not a new path fully born, but a fourth channel tearing open one brutal inch at a time until the Cycle could pour through without backing up behind three overloaded wires.

Aeric screamed into his closed teeth.

Then the scream stopped because air was more important and because the channel **held**.

> **[ CYCLE AUDIT ]**
> **Attempt:** Meridian Opening — Channel 4
> **Result:** Success — partial clearance achieved
> **Meridians Cleared:** 4 / 108
> **Note:** Stabilize before fifth attempt. Sediment burn incomplete—flush remainder before next kill-contact siphon.

“Success,” Aeric panted. “Say something nice. I dare you.”

Mariusz was quiet for a beat. When he spoke, it was almost gentle—which meant it was worse. “Keller’s scaffold would have opened that channel for him and charged interest forever. You paid in blood and grit and one dead hound. Four paths. One forest. One day. Do not waste the math.”

*Jonah. He had to say the name.*

“Primeval,” Aeric read aloud. “What’s that mean—”

“Old growth. Deep roots. Things that remember eating before your cities learned to pretend they were safe.” Mariusz’s voice thinned. For half a breath Aeric smelled something that wasn’t rot-water—chalk and wrong light stacked two ways—and then the bond snapped back to mist and blood. “Your maps are useless here. Walk like the ground is new. Because to the System, it is.”

*New ground. Old teeth. Same me, less blood.*

He stood on four open channels and a thigh that had started bleeding again, spear slick, sediment still gritting the hollow under his gut like a warning label he couldn’t peel off.

The forest ahead looked thicker. Trees grown through crushed metal half-buried in loam—car doors, maybe, or something that had been a sign before the stretch. No streetlights. No bus routes. Just primeval density closing around a man who had learned to stop before the core.

*Residue first.*

He repeated it like a prayer he didn’t believe in yet and moved anyway—deeper, toward whatever came next, while behind his eyes Mariusz counted sediment grains and said nothing about the light that wasn’t Earth’s.

Not yet.

That was a cost for later.
