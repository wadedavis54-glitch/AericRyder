# Chapter 6 — Bone Edge

The hello didn't come back.

Aeric kept walking anyway—fern line, downslope, away from the voice that had died mid-word. His leaf wrap sat heavy against his ribs. Half a cooked eel. A luxury that felt obscene after the half-step he'd almost taken toward someone else's scream.

*Could've been anyone. Wasn't. Isn't.*

Jonah's face tried to load behind his eyes—the Weaver kid choking on beetles while Aeric ran the math and left. Different forest. Same ledger.

*You don't get to be the guy who answers hello until you're not the guy who dies answering hello.*

Mariusz said nothing. The silence felt like agreement and indictment in the same breath.

---

The corpse found him before he found it.

Not fresh—wrong in the way meat went wrong when the forest had already filed its claim. A man in torn office slacks and a belt that had once held a badge clip, sprawled where two root-walls pinched a game trail. Throat opened. Not clean. Rib cage showing through a shirt that had been white.

No Interface name. No Scan worth burning focus on a dead man who wasn't moving.

Scavengers had the rest.

Three **Aether-Warped Carrion Beetles**—same species as the pack from two days that felt like a week—worked the soft parts with mandibles that clicked like bad typing. Level two, maybe three on the big one. They didn't look up until his shadow crossed the fern edge.

*Pack math. Again.*

Aeric stopped. Spear up. Perception—new half-second or not—counted angles. One on the throat. One at the left arm. One inside the rib gap, head deep, ventral plates lifted just enough to show the seam Mariusz had drilled into his skull.

**[Scan]**

*Feeding. Committed. Same homework.*

He didn't move toward the body. Moving toward the body was how you became the next soft part.

The big one—the one with its head in the rib cage—twitched a mandible free and turned one compound eye toward him.

*Not yours. Not yet.*

Aeric backed into the ferns. Slow. Blood scent already on him from the eel. Adding a corpse fight here was stupid—noise, ichor, more beetles from the smell.

The beetle watched. Decided he wasn't worth the calories. Went back to the ribs.

*There. That's the answer you didn't give the hello.*

He circled wide—upwind as best he could guess, root-mat swallowing his footfalls. The dead man's shoes were still on. One hand missing below the wrist. The forest had been busy.

*Commute clothes. Badge belt. Someone's dad. Someone's Cohen.*

He didn't look at the face. Looking was a debt he couldn't pay.

---

Half a mile on—if miles still meant anything—the deadwood spear haft bit his palm wrong.

Splinters. Blood-slick grip. Three fights and a fire drill had turned the branch into a liability. His hands were cut in places that hadn't existed on the bus.

"Project three," Mariusz said. "Tools. You have a point. You need an edge that isn't borrowed wood, and you need to stop shredding your palms every time you kill something."

"I don't have a forge."

"You have bone, patience, and a scholar who watched Vellath butchers disassemble things that would eat you. Forge is later. **Edge** is today."

Aeric knelt by a fallen log where something had dug—fresh scat, segmented, oily.

"Track," Mariusz said. "Thornbacks leave burrows. They're vermin with a dorsal that cuts cloth and a throat that's soft if you can get under the chin. Level two. Stupid when hungry. Useful when dead."

*Useful when dead. Story of my life.*

---

The burrow mouth showed at the base of a root-wall—packed mud, scratch marks, smell like wet copper and rot.

**[Scan]**

<blockquote class="status-block">

<p><strong>Entity:</strong> Aether-Warped Thornback Vermin</p>

<p><strong>Level:</strong> 2</p>

<p><strong>Threat:</strong> Burrow ambush — dorsal spine ridge; soft throat; burst / bite / retreat</p>

</blockquote>

It came out on its own schedule—not when he wanted, when **it** wanted.

Low. Fast. A bristled tube of muscle with a spine ridge like a saw blade and a mouth built for gnawing root-mat and softer things. It hit his shin—not the eel rake, fresh pain, bright and stupid—and **burst** for his calf.

Aeric dropped weight the way Wade had trained him to—*don't stand tall for the bite*—and pinned the vermin's shoulder with his knee.

*Mount. Control the hips. Don't rush.*

Rusty. Ugly. Good enough.

The dorsal ridge ground against his jeans. Cloth tore. Skin next. He grunted, shifted angle, and drove the spear **under** the chin where Scan said throat was soft.

Give.

Black ichor. The vermin thrashed—dying, not dead.

Separate beat.

Palm off the wood. Fingers under the jaw. Bare skin on the soft seam where the spine ridge didn't reach. Direct contact. Deliberate.

The rush hit—dark, warm, **good**, a pulse of yes that made his breath catch wrong. Life-Aether fled the meat before it could soak into the mud. Three beats. Four. Something **dense** pulsed under the skin—the core-knot.

He **ripped back**.

Cold where heat had been. Wrong. Empty.

*Residue. Not the seed. When do I stop leaving money on the corpse?*

Thin draw. Outward spin. Six paths. Vent.

Friction burn—molten-wire drag through the channel behind his solar plexus. Three small coughs of wrong taste. The hollow stopped **crunching**.

*Craft. Then food. Then whatever wants me for dinner.*

---

"You don't know this yet. Listen."

Aeric stopped with the corpse at his feet and listened.

"**Bone edge.** Not every bone works. Long bones split wrong. Jaw and front leg of a vermin—dense enough, small enough. You want a blade three fingers wide, one thick, longer than your thumb. **No glands**—strip anything near the head junction. Wash in boiled water if you have it. You have half a cup left—spend it on the blade, not your throat."

He didn't have fire here. He had the reserve from the bus—tepid now, but cleaner than mud.

"Step one. **Separate the jaw.** Break at the joint with rock on rock—not your spear. Step two. **Split the flat** along the natural line with a sharp flake. You won't have a flake yet—use a bus shard. Step three. **Scrape** the inner face until it catches skin. Step four. **Sinew wrap** for a handle—strip tendon from the foreleg, twist wet, dry tight. Ugly. Functional."

*Wade would call this caveman shit. Wade would also say finish the drill.*

It took an hour.

The jaw came free with a crack that sounded like something small dying again. Bus metal shard peeled a flat. Scrape, scrape, test on his arm hair—**catch**. Not pretty. **Sharp**.

Sinew from the foreleg twisted wet between his palms, wrapped around the bone base, bit into itself when it dried. A handle. A grip that wasn't splintered deadwood.

Bone knife in one hand. Spear in the other.

*First tool I made. Not the last thing I'll steal from something dead.*

"Grip wrap next," Mariusz said. "Your spear haft. Strip bark fiber from dead vine on the root-wall—soak in the last boiled water, twist cord, bind below the head. Tight. Overlap. Less blood slip."

He worked until his fingers cramped. Cord bound. Grip solid.

*Better. Still not a black belt's day. Close enough.*

The vermin meat was ranker than eel—stringy, dark. He didn't have fire on this slope. He wrapped a strip in clean-ish leaf anyway, same as the eel protocol Mariusz had hammered in.

*Eat before next night. Raw is lottery. Cook when you can.*

---

"Channel seven," Mariusz said when the bone knife sat in his belt and the sediment itch was a whisper. "While the Cycle is warm. Seventh clog sits lateral—left side, rib line. You will want to shy right because of the arm wounds. **Don't.**"

Aeric sat spine to root. Spear across thighs. Bone knife handle against his palm—real edge, real leverage.

"Ambient draw. Thin. Six paths."

Easy air. Neutral.

"Find the seventh knot. Deeper. Body's inventory of excuses is running out."

The Eye found it—a tight twist under his left ribs, mean as the sixth had been.

"Push. Friction pain. Not leech. Not sediment. Hold—"

Pain.

Wire through meat that had never meant to carry load. Birth-wrong. Mechanical.

Stubborn Will climbed. Mariusz counted. Aeric pushed on three, **held** on four when every instinct said quit.

The knot **cracked**.

Passage tore open. Seventh channel held. Cycle poured through without backing up behind six overloaded wires.

He breathed through his teeth until air mattered more than the memory of pain.

"Ten is where the cage admits you might be more than a corpse with opinions. You're not there. **Edge** is. Use the knife. Don't cut yourself proving you're a craftsman."

*Fair.*

---

The light thinned wrong again—no sunset, just dimmer green.

He found a seep smaller than the bus trough—barely a thread down moss. Not enough to boil without wasting fuel he didn't have banked here. He marked it mentally and kept moving.

*Water map. Growing. Still not enough.*

Metal glinted under fern—half a bicycle frame, wheel gone, chain rusted into the loam like the world had swallowed someone's exercise habit.

*Wrong scale. Wrong everything.*

Somewhere behind him, far enough that Perception couldn't parse words, something screamed once and stopped.

Human or not-human. The forest didn't clarify.

Aeric didn't turn back.

*Craft is just organized theft from things that already lost.*

Mariusz didn't answer that one either.

Aeric walked toward the scream's echo and did not hurry.
