# Chapter 3 — Sediment

The forest didn't get quieter as he walked.

It got *organized*.

Drip. Click. Something heavy moving through the brush. Not him.

Aeric kept the deadwood spear low. He tried to bleed quietly. His body didn't think that was funny. Every step tore open the half-healed cuts on his forearm and ribs. The shin rake from the beetle fight had gone stiff and hot. It pulled wrong when he bent his knee.

*Walk. Don't limp louder than the things hunting me. Great plan.*

The hollow under his gut—the Dantian, if he was going to use Mariusz's word for it—didn't feel empty anymore.

It felt **gritty**.

Not pain. Not the dark rush from touching dying meat. Something worse. Sand dragged through a wound that was supposed to hold fuel. It clogged the three narrow paths he'd torn open in the void, like hair stuck in a drain.

*What the fuck is that.*

"Impure Aether Sediment," Mariusz said. His voice sat behind Aeric's eyes, dry as chalk. "You gorged on a dying beetle. You almost ate a core whole. Low-tier kills leave sludge when you drink past the spill. It clogs what little you've opened. Keep siphoning like that and your meridians will rupture before your ambition does."

Aeric's jaw tightened.

*Sediment. Of course. Why wouldn't the apocalypse have sand in the fuel tank.*

"Can I—"

"Burn it off. Manual cycling. The flush hurts—not the draw. No shortcut. You want the devouring path, you learn to digest what you steal, or choke on it."

Analytical Eye tried to map it: Cur trickle, beetle flood, overdraw on the knot he'd almost taken. Two kills. Two rushes that felt too good. One price he hadn't seen coming.

*Residue first. He said that after the beetle. Thanks for the post-game tutorial.*

"How long before I'm a clogged pipe?" Aeric muttered.

"Long enough to make one more mistake if you're stupid. Short enough that I would not plan a nap."

*Cool. Cool cool cool.*

---

He found a fold in the ground—a shallow wash between two root-walls, knee-deep in cold water that smelled of iron and rotted leaf. Not clean. Better than standing in his own blood scent on open ground.

Aeric crouched behind the upstream root and looked at his ruined palm. Splinters still there, black at the edges. Infection waiting. No bandages. No soap. Just copper-thick air and a demon scholar who thought pain was homework.

*Later.*

He reached for the hollow under his gut.

"Stop." Mariusz's voice cut sharp. "You don't know this yet. Listen."

Aeric froze. Hand still on his stomach.

"Step one. Find the hollow. You did that in the void—you know where it lives. Good."

*Yeah. I know where it lives.*

"Step two. Pull ambient Aether. Thin breath, not a gulp. Grease-thick air into the hollow through the three open paths. The draw itself does not hurt. It never has. Do not confuse drinking the air with flushing the mud."

Aeric pulled.

Ambient Aether slid in—cool, neutral, easy. No corpse under his hand. No dying warmth. No reward-rush. Just air scraped down channels that were already half full of **grit**, and that part was fine. That part felt like the void tutorial, only quieter.

*Not pain. He's right. This isn't pain.*

"Step three. Do not pull more. The sediment is already inside you. Pulling harder just packs more sludge in."

Aeric stopped drawing. The easy flow cut off. The grit stayed.

"Step four. Spin what you have. Push the Cycle—not faster inward, *outward*. Force the sludge through the three open meridians and vent it. *That* is where the burn lives. Not the air. The mud."

He forced the Cycle.

Heat. Friction. Molten-wire drag as sediment fought to stay where it was choking him. Stubborn Will climbed the damage load and kept him conscious. Not a living thing to leech—worse. Dead weight with no mind, no fleeing life to catch.

*Push. Vent. Burn the mud, not the air.*

Somewhere in the grind, one grainy blockage **broke**.

Not a new meridian opening—a clog giving way inside a path he'd already paid for. The release was small and ugly, like spitting something that had no right to be in his mouth. The water in the wash rippled though nothing had touched it.

"Partial," Aeric breathed. "Love partial. Partial is my favorite word today."

Mariusz's exhale was almost amusement. Almost. "Partial keeps you alive. A full flush would have taken an hour you do not have with that scent trail behind you. You cleared enough to move. Do not confuse survival with clean."

*Scent trail. Right. I'm a walking lunch menu.*

A sound answered from downslope—wet snuffle, claws on stone, something big enough to make the root-wall shake under his palm.

Not clicking beetles.

One set of legs.

---

**[Scan]**

</blockquote>

*Level two. Solo. Soft gut. Same homework as everything else that wants to kill me.*

The hound came around the root-bend like it owned the wash—shoulders corded wrong, spine plated with bark-colored bone, muzzle wet with old blood that wasn't Aeric's. It smelled him and went still.

For one heartbeat they measured each other.

No pack math this time. No Jonah Keller bleeding luminous cords against a trunk. No second monster to pick the easier meal.

Just him, the spear, and a body already half-used.

*Residue first. Leave the core. Don't drink the mud.*

The hound lunged.

He didn't meet it square—that was how office-shirt man lost his leg across the ravine. Aeric slid downslope into the cold wash, water exploding around his knees, and let the hound's mass overshoot on slick stone. Stubborn Will turned the forearm tear into data. The Eye found the seam: belly, soft, where the armored spine didn't wrap.

He drove deadwood up under the ribs.

Not deep enough first pass—the hound twisted, jaws snapping air where his face had been, claw raking his outer thigh and opening a fresh hot line through jeans and skin. Aeric snarled, planted a boot against stone, and **shoved**.

The spear punched through.

Wet give. A stink like opened gut and copper. The hound bucked, trying to bite the wood still inside it. Aeric rode the motion down into the wash until the beast's hindquarters hit the bank and stopped fighting gravity.

Dying. Not dead yet.

The gap opened—the same stupid instinct his hand had learned on the Cur without permission.

This time he **chose** it.

Palm flat against the soft seam around the spear. Fingers sinking into hot wet fur and ruined meat. Bare skin. Direct contact. Separate from the killing blow.

Null Cycling woke through the touch.

The rush hit—dark, warm, *good*, a bloom of yes behind his sternum that made his teeth want to grin at the wrong moment. Life-Aether fled the hound's gut and poured up his arm before it could scatter into the wash. For three heartbeats it felt like the beetle again. Like the first hit of something he already knew he'd chase.

*Stop. Residue. Not the knot. Not the—*

Something denser pulsed under his palm—a seed, a core, the hard little knot Mariusz had warned him about.

Aeric **ripped his hand back** before his Dantian could lunge.

The pull cut off mid-rush. Withdrawal snapped through him like cold after heat—wrong, empty, almost grief—and the hound shuddered once and went still.

He sat in the wash breathing hard, water pink around his knees, palm shaking with want.

*I stopped. I actually stopped.*

"Good," Mariusz said, and the word was tight with something that might have been relief if he were the type. "That is control—not virtue. You left the core because I told you to, and because you still want meridians tomorrow. The residue alone bought you fuel without another sediment flood. Remember the difference. Residue is spill. The core is foundation. Foundation Siphon is when you learn to take the seed without drinking the mud. You are not there yet."

*Foundation Siphon. File that. Don't touch the knot. Got it.*

The hound's body cooled in the wash. Aeric didn't look at its eyes.

He looked at his Interface instead. Numbers were easier than what his hand still wanted.

"Everything does," Mariusz said. "Now open the fourth channel while the Cycle is still hot. You failed once behind a root wall. Failure is data. Repetition is choice. I will walk you through it again."

*Channel four. The one that told me no.*

---

He dragged himself onto the bank, out of the pink water, back against the root-wall. Spear across his thighs. Palm still twitching with the ghost of the rush.

"Step one," Mariusz said. "Ambient draw. Thin. You know this part now."

Aeric pulled. Ambient Aether slid in—easy, neutral. The sediment grit still scraped the insides of the three open paths, but the air itself didn't hurt.

"Step two. Find the fourth seam. Scar tissue wrapped around clog—the channel that refused you before."

The Eye found it. A knot of old lock wrapped around glass-hard blockage.

"Step three. Push. Not pull. Meridian Opening is friction pain—separate from the leech-high, separate from sediment burn. Too fast and something ruptures. Too slow and the mud resettles. I will count."

*Push.*

Pain arrived with opinions.

Not the leech-high. Not molten-wire reconstruction. Meridian friction—pure, mechanical, like forcing a route through meat that had been locked since birth.

Stubborn Will climbed. The Eye tracked pressure.

"Hold—now—"

The clog **cracked**.

Fire ran from the hollow under his gut up through his chest—not a new path fully born, but a fourth channel tearing open one brutal inch at a time until the Cycle could pour through without backing up behind three overloaded wires.

Aeric screamed into his closed teeth.

Then the scream stopped because air mattered more, and because the channel **held**.

*Jonah. He had to say the name.*

"Primeval," Aeric read aloud. "What's that mean—"

"Old growth. Deep roots. Things that remember eating before your cities learned to pretend they were safe." Mariusz's voice thinned. For half a breath Aeric smelled something that wasn't rot-water—chalk and wrong light stacked two ways—and then the bond snapped back to mist and blood. "Your maps are useless here. Walk like the ground is new. Because to the System, it is."

*New ground. Old teeth. Same me, less blood.*

He stood on four open channels and a thigh that had started bleeding again. Spear slick. Sediment still gritting the hollow under his gut like a warning he couldn't peel off.

The forest ahead looked thicker. Trees grown through crushed metal half-buried in loam—car doors, maybe, or something that had been a sign before the stretch. No streetlights. No bus routes. Just primeval density closing around a man who had learned to stop before the core.

*Residue first.*

He repeated it like a prayer he didn't believe in yet and moved anyway—deeper, toward whatever came next, while behind his eyes Mariusz counted sediment grains and said nothing about the light that wasn't Earth's.

Not yet.

That was a cost for later.
