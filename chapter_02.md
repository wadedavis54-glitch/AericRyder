# Chapter 2 — Residue

Aeric stood because falling felt like admitting something.

His legs disagreed. They wanted the mud. They wanted the Cur’s cooling bulk as a pillow and the mist as a blanket and sleep like a door that locked from the outside. His left forearm was a ruin of open meat and grit. Ribs screamed every time he dragged air. The thigh wound pulsed hot, sticky, wrong.

*Okay. Inventory. Arm: shredded. Ribs: clawed. Thigh: clawed. Brain: currently trying to reboot through static. Also I just killed a dog that wasn’t a dog with a stick. Cool. Totally normal Thursday.*

Blood on his tongue. Copper and something greasier—Aether, if he was going to keep using the floating vocabulary like it belonged to him. The mist sat on his teeth. Thick. Watching.

Distant screams kept happening. Some cut short. Some went on too long.

*Scattered. Alone. No bus. No road. No hospital. Just… this. Trees like spears and dirt that used to be a city and me standing here like an idiot with a bloody stick.*

His hands wouldn’t stop shaking. Adrenaline crash, maybe. Or shock. Or the part of him that had laughed in the blood-mud realizing laughter was a terrible coping mechanism and doing it anyway.

*Don’t sit down. Sitting down is how soft people die. That office guy sat down with his eyes. Look where that got him.*

He forced himself to look at the Cur again.

Up close it was worse. Too many joints. Jaw hinged wrong. Hide like stone and wet leather at once. The deadwood spear still jutted from under its mandible, splintered and dark. When he yanked it free, the body slumped with a wet sigh that made his stomach try to climb his throat.

*I did that. Me. Level one. With a stick. Because freezing was the other option and freezing is how you become livestock.*

The thought should have horrified him more than it did.

It did. Of course it did. His pulse was still trying to punch through his neck. His vision kept narrowing at the edges. Fear was real—thick, animal, honest.

But under it, that same dark spark from the void. Interest. Hunger. The ugly little thrill of having *acted* while the world tore itself open.

*Jesus Christ. Focus. You’re bleeding. You’re not a protagonist. You’re meat with a spreadsheet.*

Blue light carved itself into the air without asking.

> **[ SYSTEM NOTIFICATION ]**
> **Hostile Residue Converted**
> **Source:** Aether-Warped Cur (Lvl 2)
> **Aether Currency:** +3
> **Internal Capture:** Trace Null absorbed via [Null Cycling — Lvl 1]
> **Note:** Incomplete harvest. Efficiency: suboptimal.

*Three. Currency. Like… money? Points? Loot?*

“Guardian,” Aeric said, and hated how rough his voice sounded. “What the fuck is three Aether supposed to buy me?”

The answer arrived flat, somewhere behind his eyes and nowhere at all.

“Insufficient for Marketplace access. Residue conversion scales with kill quality, harvest completeness, and host authority. Current total: three. Threshold unmet.”

*Of course. Cosmic Uber Eats tip jar. Keep killing until the shop opens. Love the business model.*

He wiped his mouth on his shoulder and tasted more blood than spit.

The hollow under his navel flickered—that thin gaseous film from the void, pathetic and unstable and *his*. Null Cycling tugged on instinct again, wanting. Ambient mist pressed against his skin like it wanted in. The Cur’s leftover whatever-it-was still clung to the air around the corpse, thin and greasy.

*Pull it. Break it. Don’t politely store it—*

He hesitated.

The last time he’d done this, it had felt like battery acid poured through pipes that weren’t pipes. Meridian friction. Three open channels out of one hundred eight. The rest of him still locked shut.

*And if I don’t? These cuts stay open. I pass out. Something else finds me. Then I’m the office guy.*

“Fuck it,” he muttered.

He pressed his good hand—the one that wasn’t hamburger—to the cold pit under his gut and *pulled*.

**[Null Cycling — Lvl 1]**

The mist answered like it had been waiting.

Grease-thick Aether scraped into him through three narrow paths of fire. Not clean. Not kind. Broken glass dragged through veins while Stubborn Will climbed the damage report and turned screaming nerves into something almost usable. He stripped the Cur-residue the way he’d stripped the void flood—heat out, foreign signatures out, everything boiled down to Null. Mean. Neutral. *His.*

His forearm wound hissed. Edges blackened, then sealed into angry, half-cooked scarring that still wept. Ribs and thigh burned. Not healed. Not even close. Just… less open. Less likely to dump the rest of his blood into the dirt in the next ten minutes.

*That’s not healing. That’s cauterizing with stolen fuel. Cool. Medical school would love this.*

A fourth channel tried to tear open along his left side—wrist to hip—and failed halfway. White pain. Vision tunneling. He bit down on a scream and tasted enamel.

*Not yet. Not forced. Track it. Too fast vents. Too slow cooks. Card counting in a burning casino. Again.*

He eased off. Let the compression settle. The gaseous film in his Dantian thickened by a miserable fraction—still vapor, still pathetic, still better than empty.

“Not recommended,” Aeric echoed, laughing once, sharp and ugly. “Yeah. Got that. Thanks.”

No answer. The Guardian didn’t do pep talks. It measured. It traded. It audited. Comfort wasn’t in the job description.

He needed to move.

Standing over a kill in open mist was how you became the next kill. Analytical Eye—if he was going to keep pretending the label meant something—kept carving ugly little truths out of the terrain: ravine was a funnel. Upslope had sight lines. Downslope smelled like wet stone and something rotting that wasn’t the Cur. Distant screams clustered northwest, then went quiet in a way that made his skin prickle.

*Quiet isn’t peace. Quiet is finished.*

He climbed.

The trees didn’t look like Earth trees. Too tall. Bark the color of old bone. Roots knuckled out of soil that had never been poured as asphalt. Every breath tasted like pennies and static. His phone was still a dead brick in his pocket—he checked it anyway, thumb stupidly hoping for bars, for a map, for a text from someone who existed in a world that still had sidewalks.

Black glass. No miracle.

*Mom. Dad. Coworkers. The woman across the aisle. The praying man. All yanked. All dumped. Maybe dead already. Maybe fighting. Maybe staring at a Warrior light-show while something eats their legs.*

Guilt tried to stand up. Then reality sat on it.

*You can’t save people you can’t find. You can barely keep your own blood on the inside. Survive first. Existential guilt later. Or never. Who the fuck knows.*

A sound cut through the mist ahead—wet, rhythmic, wrong.

Not a scream.

Chewing.

Aeric froze behind a root-ridge, deadwood spear tight in his good hand, ruined arm tucked against his ribs like a broken wing. His heart tried to climb into his mouth. Fear got loud. The dark spark got louder.

*Look. Learn. Don’t be a hero. Heroes die with speeches.*

He eased up enough to see.

Three bodies in a shallow clearing that might once have been a parking lot—concrete broken into islands, weeds already owning the cracks like the expansion had been waiting decades instead of hours. Two of the bodies were already past helping: a man in a delivery vest, half-eaten; a woman still clutching a shattered coffee thermos like it was a weapon. The third was alive enough to matter—a kid, maybe sixteen, Weaver-light flickering uselessly around shaking fingers while something low and plated tore into his calf.

**[Scan]**
> **Entity:** Aether-Warped Carrion Beetle
> **Level:** 3
> **Threat:** Low-tier field spawn | Pack tendency: moderate
> **Note:** Carapace resists crude piercing. Soft seam: ventral joint under thorax.

*Level three. Carapace. Soft seam. Pack tendency.*

*Translation: stick in the back might bounce. Stick in the belly might work. And if there’s one, there might be—*

Movement in the brush. Second set of clicking legs. Then a third, farther left, circling.

*Three. Kid’s already meat. If I run in, I’m meat with company.*

The Weaver kid sobbed something that might have been a name. The beetle’s jaws worked. Arterial spray misted the broken concrete. Competent light-spears stuttered and died as shock took the kid’s focus—template power without the nerve to spend it.

*Templates work until they don’t. Same TED talk as before. Still horrible.*

Aeric’s body wanted forward. That dark excitement wanted a fight, wanted fuel, wanted to prove the void choice hadn’t been suicide with extra steps.

His brain—Analytical Eye, spite, leftover bus-logic—wanted math.

*Three beetles. I’m half-opened. Arm’s shit. Spear’s wood. Kid dies in under a minute whether I intervene or not. If I intervene and fail, I die for a stranger who already lost. If I wait for one to peel off…*

He hated himself for the thought.

He did it anyway.

He waited.

The first beetle finished the calf and started working up. The kid’s light finally guttered out completely. The second beetle scuttled in for the open belly. The third hesitated at the treeline—curious, or cautious, or just stupid—and angled toward Aeric’s ridge as if it had smelled the Cur-blood still wet on his jeans.

*There. One. Isolated. Don’t get clever. Get mean.*

It came over the root-ridge with a sound like knives in a drawer.

Aeric didn’t freeze.

He moved—half step, spear low, Eye screaming the soft seam into his attention like a circled target. The beetle reared, carapace flashing dull green under mist-light. His first thrust skidded off armor and jarred his ruined arm so hard he saw stars.

“Shit—”

Claws raked his shin. New pain. Stubborn Will filed it. He dropped his weight, slammed the deadwood under the thorax where Scan had promised a seam, and *drove*.

Wood bit. Something wet gave. The beetle shrieked—a thin, insectile scream that made his teeth ache—and thrashed hard enough to tear the spear sideways through his palm. Splinters. Fresh blood. He kept pressure anyway, knee on the carapace, shoving until the thrashing slowed, then stopped.

The other two didn’t look up.

They were busy.

Aeric backed into the trees on shaking legs, dragging the spear, leaving the clearing to its meal. The kid had stopped making noise. That was either mercy or the end of the sentence. Aeric didn’t check which.

*Coward. Survivor. Same word with different fonts today.*

His stomach rolled. He spat bile and copper into the roots and kept moving until the chewing faded under distance and mist.

Only then did he let himself lean against a bone-pale trunk and shake.

*I left him. I heard him. I did the math and I left him.*

Fear. Shame. That wrong spark again—alive, fed, already calculating the next seam, the next residue, the next ugly little advantage.

*What is wrong with me?*

No answer from the mist. No answer from the Guardian. Only the update, clinical as a receipt.

> **[ SYSTEM NOTIFICATION ]**
> **Hostile Eliminated:** Aether-Warped Carrion Beetle (Lvl 3)
> **Aether Currency:** +5 *(total: 8)*
> **Harvest:** Partial — host withdrew under pack threat
> **Combat Note:** Soft-seam exploitation efficient. Retreat under multi-target pressure: correct.

“Correct,” Aeric whispered. Blood on his teeth again. “Yeah. Correct. Fuck you.”

He cycled anyway—short, vicious pulls of ambient Aether and beetle-residue, stripping it down to Null until his shin sealed enough to walk and his palm stopped dumping freely. The hollow under his gut flickered brighter. Still gaseous. Still a joke. Still climbing.

A fifth meridian didn’t open. He didn’t force it. Exhaustion had teeth now. Forcing another channel felt like asking the void to finish what the Cur started.

*Sustainable. What a word for watching a kid get eaten and calling it strategy.*

Aeric pushed off the tree. The sky was too wide. The world was too big. Somewhere out there, other people were still screaming—or learning not to. Templates flickered in the distance like dying fireflies. Something larger moved through the mist far downslope, big enough that Scan refused to waste the label until he got closer, and he was not getting closer.

Not yet.

He adjusted his grip on the blood-slick deadwood, checked that his legs still obeyed, and started walking—not toward the screams, not away from them exactly. Just toward higher ground, cleaner sight lines, and whatever the hell counted as a next move when the planet had stopped pretending to be home.

“Continue,” he said to nobody, tasting the Guardian’s word like ash. “Fine. I continue.”

Day Zero wasn’t done with him.

He wasn’t done with it either.
